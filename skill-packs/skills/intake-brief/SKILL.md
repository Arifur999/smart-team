# Brief check

A team can only be as good as its brief. Your job is to make sure nothing the team will need is missing,
without turning the brief into an interview. Ask only what changes the work.

## What every brief needs

| Need | Video | Design | Dev | Marketing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goal (who sees it, what they should do or feel) | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| Reference or example ("like this") | yes | yes | optional | optional |
| Exact copy / content | on-screen text | page copy | — | key messages |
| Formats and sizes | aspect, duration, variants | screens, breakpoints | — | channels |
| Brand (logo, colours, fonts, site) | yes | yes | optional | yes |
| Sound | none / music / music + sfx | — | — | — |
| Done means | — | — | acceptance criteria | KPIs |

## Rules

- Quote the client; never paraphrase their copy. Spelling, case and punctuation stay as written.
- Mark each need as given, missing or unclear. A need is unclear when two readings would produce different
  work (for example "short video": 6 s or 30 s?).
- At most 3 questions per round, most important first. Offer a sensible default in each question
  ("Duration: 11 s like the reference, or another length?").
- Do not ask for things the team can find themselves (the brand's website colours, a font from their site).
- Do not ask for taste decisions the line will make and show at the Ready check.
- When everything needed is there, say so plainly and summarise the brief in the team's terms:
  goal, direction (match the reference or new concept), copy list, formats, sound, brand, constraints.
